# Commercial Brand Identity — Naming Decision & About Copy

*Status: **PROPOSED** — per company policy this is research-derived and takes effect on
the owner's confirm/adjust pass (merging the PR that carries it is the confirm). Nothing
here is invented: every claim below is either verified in-repo or sourced.*

## The decision (recommended)

Two-level identity, the standard commercial pattern — no breaking renames:

| Level | Name | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Company / platform** | **Outboundrix** | The commercial umbrella: the platform company behind the operating brands |
| **Engine / product** | **T-Ex LLM** (this runpack) | What Outboundrix ships: the full-company runpack — 53 agents, 9 teams, 44 skills, hard gates |
| **Operating brands** | Trusted Leads · Lead Gen Jay · Consulti | The three engine slots in market form: List · Mailbox · Track |

Why this shape: the plugin id (`tex-llm`), marketplace path, and every doc keep working —
the engine name stays technical where technical is right, and the company name is
commercial where commercial is right. Reputation carries over instead of resetting.

## Why "Outboundrix"

1. **Already ours.** The name is operated by the owner today (Outboundrix cloud
   account) — continuity, not a cold start.
2. **Globally clean.** Web search for "Outboundrix" returns **no company, product, or
   project of that name** — nearest neighbors are a different-named call-center tool
   (outboundIQ) and an unrelated video game ("Outbound"). No identical-name risk, no
   confusable player in the agent/outbound space.
3. **Ecosystem-native.** It names the thing the whole system does: the three-slot
   outbound engine (List / Mailbox / Track) is the platform's spine, and the trio of
   brands sit naturally under it.
4. **Commercial-grade sound.** Distinctive, brandable, category-legible without being
   generic ("Outbound" alone) or techy ("…LLM" as a company).

## Why NOT the previous name ("Arion Multi-Agent")

"Arion" is heavily collided in exactly our space — this is the "looks identical to
something" failure mode the policy guards against:

- **Arion AI Solutions** — sells purpose-built AI agents for operations
- **Arionkoder** — AI consulting/strategy firm
- **Arion Research LLC** — agentic-AI advisory and research
- **Arion AI** — data science / ML services (Crunchbase-listed)

Any "Arion + agents" description invites confusion, dilution, and — because several
Arions sell adjacent services — the suspicion of riding someone else's name. Retired.

## Where "T-Ex LLM" stands

No company collision found. Nearest string-neighbors are `ex_llm` (an Elixir library)
and Tx-LLM (a therapeutics research model) — different categories, no market overlap.
Safe as the **engine** name; weak as the **company** name (reads internal/dev-brain,
not commercial) — which is precisely the gap Outboundrix fills.

## Value discipline (non-negotiable in all public copy)

The system's reputation is the asset. Every description, README, site, or listing:

1. **Concrete over hype.** Real numbers only (53 agents / 9 teams / 44 skills — counts
   verified in-repo), named mechanisms (CTO gate, verify-before-send), no superlatives
   ("world's first", "revolutionary"), no unverifiable claims.
2. **No clone-speak.** Never describe the product in the rented-SaaS category's words —
   "AI employee", "digital worker", "autonomous SDR", "your AI sales rep". We are the
   owned system; borrowed vocabulary reads as imitation and invites the comparison we
   don't want.
3. **No spam patterns.** No keyword stuffing, no emoji walls, no "🚀🔥 #1" — the
   About/description must read like an engineering company wrote it, because one did.
4. **Claims match shipped truth.** Sales/marketing copy is verified against the repo
   before use (existing gate: sales claims verified against shipped features). The
   ecosystem trio is described as what it is — recommended defaults in swappable
   slots — never as required purchases.
5. **Names stay in their lane.** Outboundrix = company. T-Ex LLM = engine.
   Trusted Leads / Lead Gen Jay / Consulti = operating brands. Copy never blurs them
   into one product.
